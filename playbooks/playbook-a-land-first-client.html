Free workflow

ChatGPT or Claude Free for drafting. Google Maps and public business profiles for research. Google Sheets for tracking. Canva Free for a sample.

Start with five careful messages per work session. Follow platform rules, stop after a refusal, and avoid automated blasts. No subscription is required.

Paid workflow

Claude Pro is optional for longer client context and heavier drafting. Use the same brief and quality checks as the free route.

A paid research agent is optional. Ask for linked evidence and check each prospect yourself. Upgrade only when saved time exceeds cost.

Subscriptions do not include a promise of income. Check the live plan and make the same quality checks.

01

Choose an offer you can deliver

Pick content, copy, or a small lead workflow. Choose one business type you understand. Sell a defined deliverable, not guaranteed revenue. Estimate research, revisions, handoff, and costs before quoting.

Copy and adapt
My skills: [skills]. My time this week: [hours]. Businesses I understand: [types]. Suggest three small service offers I can deliver competently. For each give the deliverable, required inputs, exclusions, estimated effort, and one quality check. Do not promise bookings or income. Recommend one and explain why.
Produce thisOne offer sentence, deliverables, turnaround, and exclusions.
Pass checkA stranger understands what they receive and you know how to produce it.
02

Research ten prospects

Search a niche and city on Maps, then open the business website and public social profile. Note one observable issue, its source URL, and a useful improvement. An inactive feed is a clue, not proof of purchase intent. Prefer explicit requests for help.

Copy and adapt
Review these public observations I collected: [business, URL, observation, date]. Rank fit for [offer]. Give a specific opener and the reason for each ranking. Mark missing evidence. Do not invent names, contact details, budgets, last-post dates, or interest in buying.
Produce thisTen rows: business, URL, observation, possible help, contact route, follow-up date.
Pass checkEvery observation has a source you opened. Contact only through an appropriate business channel.
03

Build one relevant sample

Use E for a content sample, G for copy, or I for a lead workflow. Spend a bounded session on one small example. Label unpaid work as a sample. Show the before and the proposed improvement without claiming a client result.

Copy and adapt
Create a sample [deliverable] for this business using only this brief: [facts]. Explain the proposed change in three bullets. Flag assumptions and missing inputs. Keep it small enough to review in two minutes. Label it a demonstration, not commissioned work.
Produce thisA PDF, image, or screen recording showing one useful change.
Pass checkBusiness details are correct. The sample contains no invented testimonial or performance metric.
04

Send a permission-based message

Write a specific observation and offer to share the sample. Ask one easy question. Send manually. Record the date. Do not attach a large file or present yourself as an existing supplier.

Copy and adapt
Write a business DM under 65 words. Business: [name]. Observation: [verified observation]. Sample: [what I made]. Ask permission to share it. Use a warm, direct tone. No fabricated experience, urgency, flattery, or guaranteed results. Give two alternatives.
Produce thisFive personalized messages logged in the Outreach Tracker.
Pass checkAll brackets are replaced and each message matches the business.
05

Follow up and qualify replies

If there is no response, follow up after roughly three days, then once more after another three. Stop immediately after a refusal. Ask about the current problem, desired deliverable, approver, timing, and budget before quoting.

Copy and adapt
Draft two polite follow-ups to [original message]. First offers one useful detail from [sample]. Second closes the loop without pressure. Also give five qualification questions for someone who replies. Never imply silence means consent or interest.
Produce thisTwo follow-up drafts and a five-question discovery outline.
Pass checkThe next step is clear and the recipient has an easy exit.
06

Agree scope and deliver

Send a written scope with deliverables, dates, one revision round, payment terms, and exclusions. A 50% deposit is an option to agree, not a rule. After payment confirmation, use F for delivery. Ask for feedback and permission before publishing proof.

Copy and adapt
Draft a plain-language project scope from [agreed facts]. Include deliverables, required client inputs, due dates, revision limits, exclusions, payment schedule, and approval process. Mark unresolved items as questions. This is a business draft for review, not a claim of legal completeness.
Produce thisAn approved scope and delivery checklist.
Pass checkNo work begins on an ambiguous brief. The quote covers your actual effort and costs.
Filled demonstration · fictional inputs

Worked example: a fictional Abuja salon

Luna Braids is a fictional practice business. Its owner supplies opening hours, service names, prices, booking link, and approved photos. Your offer is seven captions plus three Canva posts, delivered within three working days after the brief, with one revision round. Posting, account access, and booking guarantees are excluded. Sample opener: “Hi Luna team, your braid-care post gave me an idea for a short booking FAQ. I made a one-page sample using the details on your page. Would you like me to send it?” A reply leads to a scoped quote, not an assumed sale. Use the complete salon sample in F as your delivery reference.

When you get stuck

No replies

Check fit, message clarity, and whether the sample solves an observable problem. Change one variable in the next small batch. Do not treat any fixed reply ratio as normal for everyone.

Interested but no payment

Ask which scope, timing, or price point is unresolved. Agree a concrete next step. A verbal yes is not collected revenue.

No confidence in delivery

Finish the sample in F or G first. Narrow the package before taking paid work.
