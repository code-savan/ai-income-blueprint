START / TOOL ROUTE
Before you begin

Start with the tools you have.

The output stays the same. Choose how you’ll produce it.

Free workflow

Start with a free writing tool and one free editor: Docs, Sheets, Slides, Canva or a video editor. Use your own files or assets you have permission to use.

Finish one practice output before buying anything. Free-tier limits can change. Save your work and test a free export first.

Paid workflow

Paid AI, design or research tools are optional. Pay for a specific problem, such as a longer source file or repeated editing task.

Read the current plan, country support and credit cost in Tools & costs. Budget for retries. A subscription is not a fixed number of finished deliverables or sales.

Check the current plan and your actual bottleneck before spending.

Choose where to look

You can find suitable clients on any relevant platform. Start with one channel and one buyer type. These are search methods, not promises of ready buyers.

Instagram / exact search steps
Open Instagram ↗
  1. Tap Search and enter a buyer phrase such as “photographer Bristol”, “online tutor” or “handmade stationery”. Try a service keyword and a location separately.
  2. Open relevant profiles, their bio link and recent posts. Read captions and comments for questions the business answers or an explicit request for help.
  3. For copy, inspect the bio, offer and enquiry page. For design, inspect readability. For video, find authorized longer videos that could become clips. For research, use only public business information.
  4. Record the profile/post URL, date and exact observation. If the account is private or evidence is inaccessible, do not claim you checked it.
  5. Use a relevant business DM if allowed. Ask permission to share a small sample. Do not mass-message followers or scrape personal details.
Copy the Instagram research prompt
Research-agent task for Instagram. My service: [service]. Exact package: [files]. Buyer type: [type]. Location: [city/country or remote]. Search constraints: Instagram profiles and public posts. Use profile/post URLs. Check the bio, offer link and visible questions. Do not guess post dates, treat low follower count as buying intent, scrape followers or access private profiles. Find up to ten relevant businesses or explicit requests. Return name, source URL, checked date, exact visible observation, potential help, permitted public business contact route and unknowns. Separate verified facts from suggested fit. Do not invent contacts, dates, budgets, buyer intent or metrics. Do not send anything. If a source cannot be opened, say so and give manual search phrases instead. Start with three rows for my review before continuing.
Facebook Groups / exact search steps
Open Facebook Groups ↗
  1. Search a buyer phrase such as “small business owners”, “freelance designers” or a local business group. Open Groups in the search results.
  2. Read About and group rules before joining or posting. Some groups prohibit promotion or unsolicited messages.
  3. Use the group’s search for real requests: “website help”, “looking for an editor”, “product descriptions” or the task your service solves.
  4. Save the permitted post URL and what the author actually asked. Answer a useful part of their question publicly where rules allow.
  5. Ask whether they want a sample only when relevant and permitted. Do not export members or DM everyone in the group.

Make a small sample they can judge

A sample is a small piece of the work you would sell. A copywriter can rewrite one headline. A designer can make one carousel. A website seller can build one demo page. Use the recipe for your chosen service below. Label it “practice demo” or “unpaid sample”. Use public facts, then ask permission before publishing someone’s brand or work.

Example for your offer

One-page business website

A useful small sample: A fictional tutor’s page with a lesson enquiry button. Label it a demo.

Complete static website starter ↓ · Rename a copy to index.html.

  1. Pick one business type you understand. Write three common customer questions and one useful next action.
  2. Open ChatGPT, Claude or Z.ai. Paste the website prompt below with those facts. Ask for one self-contained index.html file. Copy only the HTML, without markdown fences, and save it in a new folder. The included website-starter.txt is also a complete editable practice page. Rename a copy to index.html before opening or uploading it.
  3. On Windows use Notepad → Save as → All files → index.html → UTF-8. On Mac use TextEdit → Format → Make Plain Text → save index.html without .txt. Open the file in a browser.
  4. Ask the AI to fix one problem at a time. Paste the relevant code and say what you clicked, expected and saw. Replace sample contact links before sharing.
  5. Open Cloudflare dashboard → Workers & Pages → Create application → Pages / Get started → Drag and drop. Choose a project name, upload the folder and select Deploy site. Labels may move. Use the Direct Upload source below if they differ.
  6. Open the pages.dev URL on your phone. Tap every contact link and check text at a narrow width. Send this preview to a suitable buyer, with permission.
  7. For paid delivery, agree the client-owned account, domain cost, maintenance and changes in writing. Send files and a short update guide. A static page has no working payment system or database by default.

Check your finished workEvery link works, phone text fits, facts are approved, images have rights, and a new visitor can find the contact button. No fake working form.

Prompt for this service
Build one self-contained index.html with inline CSS and minimal JavaScript for this fictional demo: [business type], [reader], [offer], [approved facts], [contact URL]. First ask for missing essential facts. Include headline, services, three FAQs, and one clear contact link. Responsive layout, semantic HTML, readable contrast and visible keyboard focus. No framework, backend, checkout, fake reviews or unsupported claims. Do not make a form appear to submit. Give the complete file, saving instructions and a link-by-link phone test. Mark fictional demo visibly.

Calling can cost money. Free alternatives are permitted manual Instagram messages, helpful group replies and relevant business conversations. Choose the route that fits your location and the recipient.

Where you arePractical starting option
United StatesCheck your local SIM/carrier plan first. Google Voice has US personal and paid standalone options. Business plans and international calls have separate terms. Don’t assume an unlimited domestic plan includes every destination.
EuropeUse your local carrier’s business/international rates, or an eligible Google Voice Workspace plan in a supported country. Not every European country is supported. Compare a prepaid international app for the exact destination.
AfricaStart with a local SIM/carrier and check its international tariff. Talk360 offers prepaid international calling. Confirm app availability, payment method and the exact origin/destination rate before topping up. It does not promise a US caller ID or a free calling plan.
  1. Open the provider’s rate page and choose your calling country and the recipient’s country. Check mobile versus landline, currency, minimum top-up and expiry.
  2. Estimate cost as rate × planned minutes, plus any subscription or connection charge shown. Example arithmetic only: 0.10 per minute × 30 minutes = 3.00 in that rate’s currency.
  3. Check business use and recipient-country calling rules, respect local hours and stop when asked. Do not record calls without checking consent requirements.
  4. Use your real identity and approved caller ID. Don’t hide your location or claim to be a local office.
