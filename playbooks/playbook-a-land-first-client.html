Playbook A Track B · Service Sales 7-day sprint

Land your first service client in 7 days

A positioning framework, the exact platforms where buyers are already signaling they need help, outreach templates that get replies, and a 3-step follow-up sequence — run start to finish, in order.

Goal
First paid client
Avg. time to reply
72 hours
Built for
Track B students

What this playbook assumes: you've run the 12-minute diagnostic and landed on Track B — you want cash flow this week, not a build-it-and-wait product. No portfolio, no following, no camera required.

The failure pattern isn't lack of skill — it's outreach that goes to the wrong 50 businesses, with a message that reads like a template. This playbook fixes both: who you target, and what you say. Most students who follow it exactly close their first client between day 7 and day 11 — some faster. If you're past day 7 and still mid-conversation, that's normal. Keep running the follow-up sequence; don't restart with a new pitch.

Day 1 Day 2 Day 3 Day 4 Day 5 Day 6 Day 7 Checklist
01
45–60 min

Pick your service, then build your positioning line

Pick one service. Not three. A prospect who sees "content, copywriting, or automation — whichever you need" reads a generalist, and generalists get ignored. The three fastest to sell and deliver with AI:

Content calendars & social management

Every local business needs a posting rhythm and almost none have time for it. Deliverable: a month of captions, hooks, and a posting schedule.

Copywriting — sites, emails, ads

High demand, fast to produce, easy to show before/after. Deliverable: a rewritten homepage, a 5-email sequence, or 3 ad variants.

Simple automation — chatbots, workflows

Sells on the "modern upgrade" pitch and carries the highest perceived value for the least of your time. Deliverable: a WhatsApp/website FAQ bot or a lead-capture workflow.

Once you've picked one, write your positioning line — the sentence that does the selling before you've said anything else. Every template below is built around this one line, so get it right first.

Positioning formula
I help [specific business type] get [specific, measurable result] without [the pain they currently have] — using AI, delivered in [fast timeframe].
Fill every bracket with something specific enough that it could only describe this one line of work — not "I help businesses grow."

Content example

"I help hair & beauty spas stay booked with daily social posts, without their owner touching a caption — a month of content, delivered in 48 hours."

Copywriting example

"I help law consultancies turn website visitors into consultation bookings, without hiring a copywriter on retainer — new homepage copy in 3 days."

Why this matters: everything downstream — your DM, your objection answers, your closing price — is just this sentence restated in a different format. Weak positioning makes every later step harder than it needs to be.
02
60–75 min

Build a list of prospects who are already looking

Don't cold-pitch random businesses — target the ones actively signaling they need help. Reply rates on a signaled prospect run several times higher than a cold blast to an unremarkable business. Build a list of 40–50 prospects across these four sources so day 3–5 outreach never runs dry.

Instagram & Facebook

Search your niche + city. Open each profile and check the last post date. Anything older than 2 weeks is a signal — they've stopped, or never had a system.

search: "[niche] [city]" → sort by last active

Google Maps

Search the service type in your target city. Prioritize listings with 3-star-or-lower reviews, no website link, or a Facebook page as their only web presence.

search: "[service] near [city]" → filter: no website

Facebook Groups

Join 3–5 local business or entrepreneur groups. Use the group's search bar for phrases like "anyone good with content" or "need a copywriter." These are people asking to be sold to.

group search: "recommend" · "anyone know a good"

LinkedIn

Search job titles — Owner, Founder, Managing Director — plus your city. Filter for posts with "too busy to post," "wearing every hat," or similar overwhelm language.

search: "founder" OR "owner" + "[city]"

Log every prospect in a simple sheet as you find them — name, platform, the specific thing you noticed about them (you'll need it for personalization), and a blank column for touch dates. You'll fill in the rest starting day 3.

03
90 min

Write and send your first 25 messages

Personalize the first line of every message — it's the whole ballgame. A generic opener gets skimmed and ignored; a specific one gets a reply because it proves you actually looked. Use the platform variant that matches where you found each prospect.

Template · Instagram / Facebook DM
Hey [name] — saw [business name] in [city] and love [specific thing you noticed]. I help [business type] like yours [specific result] using AI — takes hours, not weeks. Worth a quick 10-min call this week to see if it's a fit?
Best on Instagram and Facebook. Keep it casual — match the platform's tone.
Template · LinkedIn
Connection note 
Hi [name] — came across your post about [topic] and it resonated. I work with [business type] owners on [specific result]. Would love to connect.
Message after they accept 
Thanks for connecting, [name]. Quick one — I help business owners like you [specific result] using AI, without adding to your plate. If [specific pain] is on your radar, open to a 10-min call this week?
Two-step: connect first, pitch after acceptance. Pitching in the connection note itself kills reply rate.
Template · Facebook Group
Public comment on their post 
This is exactly what I help [business type] owners with — happy to share a couple of quick ideas if useful, no pitch, just DM me!
DM once they respond 
Hey [name], following up from the group — here's what I'd try first for [business name]: [one free, specific tip]. If you'd rather I just handle it, I do this for [business type] using AI — happy to hop on a quick call.
Leading with a public, no-pitch comment builds trust before the DM ever lands.
Rule for today: send 25, not "as many as feel comfortable." Volume is what makes tomorrow's numbers work — 25 sent, roughly 3–5 replies, 1–2 calls, is a normal, healthy ratio at this stage.
04
45–60 min

Send the remaining 25

Same templates, same personalization standard, next 25 prospects from your list. Don't slow down to "perfect" a message — a well-personalized first line sent today beats a flawless one sent next week. If you're getting zero replies from yesterday's batch, don't panic yet: day 0 sends typically don't get answered until the 48-hour mark.

Check in on yesterday's 25. Anyone who replied gets answered today, not tomorrow — response speed is itself a proof point ("this person is fast and easy to work with").

05
30–45 min

First follow-up touch, all replies answered

This is where most students quietly quit — and it's exactly where the money is. A prospect who didn't reply on day 3 isn't a no; they're busy. The 3-step sequence below runs on a fixed clock, spaced roughly 72 hours apart, and stops at message 3. No exceptions, no "just one more."

3-step follow-up sequence
Message 1 · Day 0 (your original send) 
Hey [name], saw [business type] in [city] — love what you're doing. I help same-type businesses [result] with AI. Open to a 10-min call this week?
Message 2 · Day 3, if no reply 
Hey [name], circling back — I put together a quick idea for [business name] around [result]. Want me to send it over?
Message 3 · Day 6, if still no reply 
Last one [name] — no pressure. If [result] ever becomes a priority, I'm the person for it. Keeping you top-of-mind.
3 touches maximum, ~72 hours apart. After message 3, move on — mark them "dormant" and revisit in a month, not this week.
06
30–45 min

Handle replies, send proof, book calls

Every reply gets an answer inside 24 hours — slower than that and the conversation goes cold. If you don't have a portfolio, that's fine: send one worked example built specifically for them (a sample caption week, a rewritten homepage paragraph, a mock workflow diagram). A tailored sample beats a generic portfolio every time.

Most replies aren't a "yes" — they're an objection wearing a question's clothing. Answer directly, don't over-explain.

"What's this cost?"
Depends on scope — most [service] packages start at $[price]. Want me to put together a specific quote for [business name] based on what you need?
"I need to think about it."
Totally fair. What's the main thing you're weighing — price, timing, or whether it'll actually work for [business name]? Happy to address it directly.
"Not right now."
No pressure — I'll check back in a bit. If [result] becomes a priority, I'm here.
"I already have someone."
Good to hear you've got it covered. If it's ever not delivering [result], happy to be a backup — no hard feelings either way.
07
Calls as booked

Close

Keep the call to ten minutes. Ask about their current situation for two of them, then move straight into the script — don't let it drift into an unstructured chat. Ask for a yes or a no directly; "let me think about it" from a call that already covered price and scope is usually just a soft no you haven't heard yet.

Closing script
Here's what I'd do in the first [timeframe]: [1–2–3 concrete deliverables]. Price is $[price]. I can start [day]. Fair? If not, what would make it a yes?
Ask yes/no directly. Don't chase "maybe" past this point.
Deposit ask
I ask for 50% upfront to lock the start date, with the balance due on delivery. I can send the invoice or payment link right now — want me to?
Get this in the same conversation as the yes. A verbal yes with no deposit is still a maybe.
The moment it lands: deliver fast, under-promise and over-deliver on this first project, and ask for a testimonial the instant they're happy — before the next client conversation, not after. That testimonial is what makes client two easier than client one.
