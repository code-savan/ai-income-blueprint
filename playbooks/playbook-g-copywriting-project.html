01

Choose one page and one action

Select a small project: hero section, booking page, or three follow-up emails. Ask who the reader is and what action they should take. Collect current copy, service details, genuine proof, and objection notes.

Produce thisA copy brief and three observable problems.
Pass checkThe scope names the page sections and excludes development if you are only writing.
Your working prompt
Audit this copy [copy] for [reader] seeking [goal]. List unclear promises, missing specifics, weak CTA, and claims needing evidence. Recommend one small project scope. Do not infer conversion rates or revenue lost.
02

Build a message hierarchy

Identify the buyer problem, relevant outcome, reason to trust, and next step. Use customer language from actual interviews or reviews. Never copy competitors verbatim or invent testimonials.

Produce thisA positioning outline and headline options.
Pass checkEach line has a factual basis and the claim fits the business capability.
Your working prompt
Using only [business facts and customer language], build a message hierarchy: audience, problem, offered service, differentiator, verified proof, CTA, and objections. Mark any unsupported promise. Give three headline directions.
03

Draft a before/after sample

Rewrite the hero and one supporting section. Explain what changed and why. Use deliverable-focused copy without promising an exact sales lift. Show the original and your proposed version for comparison.

Produce thisA readable before/after document.
Pass checkThe new text answers who, what, why, and next step in seconds.
Your working prompt
Rewrite this hero [original] using [approved hierarchy]. Output headline, subheadline, three benefit bullets, CTA, and a short explanation of each change. Avoid vague adjectives, invented proof, and guarantees. Label the result proposed copy.
04

Write a three-email sequence

For opted-in leads or customers, draft delivery/value, problem-and-solution, and invitation-to-act emails. Use the client’s actual consent and unsubscribe process. Do not imply cold contacts subscribed.

Produce thisThree edited emails and sending conditions.
Pass checkEach message delivers on the reason the recipient opted in.
Your working prompt
Write three follow-up emails for [consented audience] about [offer]. Email one delivers [requested resource]. Two explains a useful step. Three offers [actual next step]. Include subject, preview text, body, and CTA. No false scarcity or fabricated outcomes.
05

Review and deliver

Check names, claims, links, tone, and reading flow. Include source notes for factual claims. Ask for one approval pass. Provide a document with placement instructions and versions. If the client tests it, record the actual data and uncertainty.

Produce thisFinal copy, placement guide, and approval record.
Pass checkThe client knows where each section belongs and no placeholder goes live.
Your working prompt
QA this copy [draft] against [approved facts]. Return incorrect facts, unsupported claims, unclear wording, broken placeholders, and inconsistent CTAs. Give exact revisions. Produce a placement guide and approval questions.

When you get stuck
Client asks for a sales guarantee

Guarantee only deliverables or an agreed correction process you control. Sales depend on factors outside the copy.

No customer research

Ask for actual questions from inquiries and anonymized objection notes. Label untested hypotheses.

You are asked to build the site too

Define implementation as separate scope with its own cost and acceptance checks.
