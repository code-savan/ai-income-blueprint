01

Create one useful sample

Choose a sample with a quick task: one calendar row, a copy before/after, or a short storyboard. Keep it aligned with the paid offer. Include a next step without hiding the resource behind unnecessary questions.

Copy and adapt
Create a useful sample from [product/service]. It should solve [small task] in ten minutes and lead naturally to [paid next step]. Include a filled example, instructions, and a clear limit of scope. No exaggerated value amount.
Produce thisA sample page or file with a useful first action.
Pass checkThe sample works independently and previews the actual offer.
02

Test delivery from the buyer side

Open the sample link in a signed-out browser and test the intended access. Check mobile readability, file permissions, and access instructions. For public samples, exclude client or account details. Record what you actually tested.

Copy and adapt
Review these delivery steps [steps]. Identify permission, mobile, file-format, and broken-link risks. Write a short access message and a recovery message if the link fails. Do not claim any check was performed unless I confirm it.
Produce thisA verified sample link and fallback delivery message.
Pass checkThe resource is available to its intended audience with the intended permissions.
03

Respond to requests clearly

If a post says comment a keyword, respond manually or use a verified eligible automation flow. Offer the requested link first. Ask one relevant question after delivery. Do not add people to a marketing list without the relevant consent.

Copy and adapt
Write a manual response to someone asking for [sample]. Give [link], explain the quick task, then ask one optional question about their situation. Write a shorter public reply. No false claim of automated delivery.
Produce thisA resource response and one qualification question.
Pass checkThe user receives what the post promised.
04

Bridge to the relevant offer

Use the person’s stated need to suggest the paid product or a service conversation. State price, contents, limitations, and next step. Answer objections honestly. Respect a refusal and avoid pressure from your own deadline.

Copy and adapt
A prospect said [actual question]. My offer is [verified contents and price]. Draft a short helpful reply connecting their need to the offer. Include relevant limits. If the offer does not fit, say so. Do not invent urgency or guarantee results.
Produce thisA truthful offer reply and working checkout/proposal link.
Pass checkThe person knows exactly what they would receive and pay.
05

Measure the whole path

Track requests, successful deliveries, useful conversations, checkout visits where available, sales, and collected net receipts. Use a distinct link or UTM per content source if supported. Review where people stop before changing every post.

Copy and adapt
Here are real funnel counts [counts and date range]. Calculate request-to-delivery, delivery-to-conversation, and conversation-to-sale rates. Identify missing attribution. Suggest one improvement with a test plan. Separate small-sample observations from conclusions.
Produce thisOne funnel review and next experiment.
Pass checkRates use matched time periods and known denominators. Sales are not treated as available payouts.

When you get stuck

People ask for the link but do not buy

Find out what they wanted from the sample. Improve fit and the bridge to the offer, not pressure.

Automation is unavailable

Use the manual route. Do not advertise instant automated delivery until it works.

You need cash by a date

Check available balance, review status, withdrawal method, and processing estimate with your provider. Revenue and bank arrival are separate events.
