What this playbook assumes: you have something to promote — a Track A product from Playbook B, or a Track B service you're using this to generate inbound replies for instead of cold outreach alone.

This isn't a virality playbook. It's a conversion playbook — a content piece that gets 500 views and 3 clicks beats one that gets 50,000 views and 2 clicks. Every step below optimizes for the click, not the view count.

Step 1 Step 2 Step 3 Step 4 Step 5 Step 6 Checklist
01
Before you film anything

Nail your #1 transformation, not your features

A features list ("10 templates, 5 checklists, lifetime access") makes a viewer think. A transformation ("stop staring at a blank caption box every Sunday night") makes them feel something in under 2 seconds — and 2 seconds is your entire hook window. Write your transformation as a single before-and-after sentence before moving to Step 2.

Transformation line
Before: [specific frustration, in the audience's own words]
After: [specific, felt outcome — not a feature]
Example — Before: "Scrambling for a caption at 11pm." After: "A month of posts done before Monday."
02
Pick one to start

Choose your faceless format

Screen + voiceover

Record a voiceover, show slides or screencaps underneath. Highest conversion, simplest to produce — this is the default if you're unsure.

Text-on-screen + b-roll

Text overlaid on stock or generated footage. Fastest to produce, works well when you're batching high volume.

Storytime / "How I..."

A narrative arc — still faceless, still voiceover-driven, but framed as a personal account rather than a direct pitch.

Tool note: generate the raw visual assets in Google Flow, then refine in Canva or CapCut. Set CapCut's default export to 1080×1920 with auto-captions on — captions boost retention on nearly every faceless format.
03
The core formula

The Faceless Formula — 4 beats, every time

Every converting piece follows the same structure. Deviating from it is the most common reason a piece gets views but no clicks.

0–3s

Hook

Open on the pain point or a shocking result. "Nobody told [audience] this about [problem]." Stop the scroll before anything else happens.

3–10s

Problem

Agitate the pain. Name the frustration your audience feels daily, specifically enough that they nod without thinking.

10–20s

Solution

Introduce your product or service as the fix. Show the transformation from Step 1 — not a feature tour.

20–30s

CTA

"Link in bio." One clear action. No options, no second CTA, no confusion about what to do next.

Two full example scripts
Script A — product promo (problem-hook) 
Nobody told small business owners this about social media: posting inconsistently is worse than not posting at all.
Every gap in your feed tells new visitors you might have closed down.
That's exactly why I built the 7-Day Content Calendar — a month of ready captions in one file, no more Sunday-night scrambling.
Link in bio — it's $37.
Script B — service promo (curiosity-hook) 
I found the fastest way to double a local business's social engagement — and it takes them zero extra minutes a week.
Most owners are either posting nothing, or posting whatever they can grab five minutes before close.
I use AI to write a month of on-brand content in a single afternoon, then hand it over ready to post.
DM me "CONTENT" and I'll show you exactly how it works.
Notice both follow the identical 4-beat timing — only the words change.
04
Test before you commit

Run 3 hook styles, not 1

You won't know your winning angle until the data tells you. Make your first 3 pieces with the same solution and CTA, but 3 different hook styles:

Problem-hook

"Nobody told [audience] this about [problem]." Leads with the pain directly.

Curiosity-hook

"I found the fastest way to [result] — and it takes zero extra effort." Leads with an open loop.

Result-hook

"[Specific number/result] in [timeframe], here's exactly how." Leads with proof.

05
One session, one week of output

Batch it — don't create daily

One 5-hour batch day produces a full week of content. Daily scrambling is where consistency dies — batching removes the daily decision entirely.

Content batching prompt
I'm promoting [product/service] to [specific audience]. The #1 transformation is: [before] → [after].

Write 7 scripts using the Faceless Formula (hook / problem / solution / CTA, 30 seconds total):
- 3 using a problem-hook
- 2 using a curiosity-hook
- 2 using a result-hook

Vary the specific wording of each hook so none feel repeated. Keep the solution and CTA beats consistent across all 7. Format each as a 4-line script labeled by beat.
One prompt run, one batch day of filming, one week of scheduled posts.
06
Daily, 10 minutes

Check analytics, double down on what works

Post daily and check performance every day — not weekly. If a post beats your average views, make 2 more in that exact style before moving on to a new idea. Cross-post every winning piece to Reels and Shorts using the same file; it costs nothing and widens your funnel for free.

The loop: post → check which hook style outperformed → make 2 more in that style → repeat. Students who hit a breakout post (10k+ views) almost always found it by doubling down on data, not by guessing a new angle each day.
