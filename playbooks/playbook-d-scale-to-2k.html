01

Measure your current economics

Record collected revenue, processing fees, refunds, subscriptions, delivery hours, and outreach hours. Separate pipeline from money received. Compare net contribution per project and repeat purchase rate.

Produce thisA filled weekly scorecard and cost baseline.
Pass checkEvery number has a source and profit is not confused with gross sales.
Your working prompt
Analyze this real weekly data: [revenue, fees, refunds, tools, hours, projects]. Calculate net contribution and effective hourly return. Show formulas and missing inputs. Separate collected money, invoiced money, and pipeline. Do not forecast from one week.
02

Fix the delivery bottleneck

List steps you repeat. Standardize intake, brief, drafting, QA, and handoff using F. Time a second delivery using the template. Keep the work client-specific and do not reuse confidential material across clients.

Produce thisOne tested delivery SOP and measured time saving.
Pass checkA second project follows the process without losing quality.
Your working prompt
Turn my actual workflow [steps and durations] into an SOP. Give input, action, output, owner, and quality check for each stage. Suggest two simplifications. Do not remove client approval or verification to save time.
03

Test a better package or price

Improve scope and evidence before changing prices. Quote new work based on delivery cost, demand, and value. Honor existing agreements. Test one change rather than assuming a fixed percentage increase works.

Produce thisA revised package with a price floor.
Pass checkThe price covers the work and the promised scope fits your weekly capacity.
Your working prompt
Current offer: [scope, price, costs, hours]. Buyer feedback: [actual feedback]. Propose three revised packages with explicit inclusions, exclusions, capacity, and margin. Draft a clear new-client quote. Do not invent scarcity or ROI.
04

Offer continuity where it helps

A retainer fits recurring needs, not every project. Show what happens every month, reporting, revisions, payment schedule, and cancellation terms. Review performance with the client before proposing ongoing work.

Produce thisOne recurring proposal tied to an actual need.
Pass checkThe ongoing value is clear without repeating a one-off deliverable.
Your working prompt
Draft a recurring proposal for [client need] using [delivered work and results]. Include monthly outputs, reporting, boundaries, cancellation, price, and review date. Make no guarantee of sales or platform reach.
05

Grow within capacity

Choose one proven acquisition channel. Increase volume only while delivery stays reliable. Add another offer after the first is documented and profitable. Estimate how many clients or units reach the target and how much work they require.

Produce thisA capacity-aware growth plan and weekly review.
Pass checkYour target uses actual delivery capacity, fees, and costs, not optimistic headline math.
Your working prompt
I have [hours] per week. A client takes [hours] and pays [price], with [costs]. Calculate feasible clients, gross revenue, and net contribution. Compare with [product units and price]. Identify the bottleneck and a conservative next experiment.
Finished reference / fictional practice inputs

Worked example: four recurring clients

Planning scenario, not a student result: four clients paying $500 produce $2,000 gross monthly revenue. If direct monthly costs are $200 and total delivery plus acquisition time is 40 hours, contribution before tax and other overhead is $1,800, or $45 per hour. If revisions double the time, the hourly result halves. A smaller, clearly scoped retainer often improves reliability more than adding a second income stream immediately.

When you get stuck
Revenue rises but you are exhausted

Measure revision and communication hours. Tighten scope, improve intake, or reduce client count.

One client supplies most income

Keep prospecting and build a cash buffer. Do not treat a single renewal as stable demand.

You want to add five offers

Document one profitable workflow first. Add only the complementary offer customers repeatedly request.
